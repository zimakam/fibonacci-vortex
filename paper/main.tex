\title{Golden-ratio scale invariance and exact Burgers solutions in
Fibonacci-layered vortex circulation}

\author{Ziyavutdinov Magomed Kamalovich}
\affiliation{Independent researcher}
\email{zimakam@gmail.com}
\affiliation{ORCID: 0009-0005-9212-9921}

\date{\today}

\begin{abstract}
We study a vortex whose circulation is organised as a Fibonacci hierarchy
of $K$ concentric layers modulated by the golden ratio $\phig = (1+\sqrt{5})/2$.
The local homogeneity exponent
$\alpha(r) = -d\log\Gam/d\log r$ at the core scale satisfies
$\alpha(K) = (K-1)\log\phig/2 + c_\infty + O(1/K)$ with
$c_\infty \approx -0.0665$. Exact $\phig$-self-similarity
$\Gam(\phig r)/\Gam(r) = 1/\phig$ requires $\alpha=1$ and uniquely selects
the integer $K=5$ from $K^*=5.43$. We further show that the circulation
satisfies the generalized Burgers vortex equation with a variable strain
coefficient $a(r)/\nu = \langle k\rangle_w\,\lambda/r + 1/r^2$ (residual
$\sim 10^{-15}$), where $\lambda = \log\phig/r_c$. The corresponding
$5^{\text{th}}$-order annihilator $L_5 = \prod_{k=0}^{4}(\partial_r + k\lambda)$
factorises in a supersymmetric form. A thermodynamic analogy identifies
a four-level Schottky anomaly, and a mode-parity mirror theorem
$\Gam(r,\pi-\theta) = \Gam_{\text{alt}}(r,\theta)$ is verified to machine
precision. We relate the result to the regularised VORTEX profile used in
SPARC rotation-curve fitting and to a counter-rotating tetrahedral
(Merkaba) architecture, where an exact identity $\lambda = s^2\sqrt{5}/c^2$
emerges from the same golden-ratio structure.
\end{abstract}

\maketitle

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

The golden ratio $\phig = (1+\sqrt{5})/2$ appears across scales in nature,
from phyllotaxis to quantum spectra. In fluid dynamics, self-similar vortex
structures are typically associated with power-law profiles. The classical
Burgers vortex provides an exact solution of the Navier--Stokes equations
with constant strain, and the Lamb--Oseen vortex describes viscous diffusion
of an initially concentrated core.

In this paper we ask a specific question: if the circulation of a vortex
core is organised as a Fibonacci hierarchy of $K$ concentric layers with
$\phig$-weighted amplitudes, does the resulting profile exhibit exact
$\phig$-self-similarity? We show that it does, but only for one integer
$K$: $K=5$. This integer is not a free parameter but the unique value
at which the local homogeneity exponent crosses unity. We derive a closed
analytic formula for $\alpha(K)$, establish an exact identity between the
Fibonacci circulation and the generalized Burgers equation, and verify
all results numerically to machine precision.

Section~\ref{sec:model} defines the model. Section~\ref{sec:alpha} derives
the analytic formula for $\alpha(K)$. Section~\ref{sec:Kstar} shows the
selection of $K=5$. Section~\ref{sec:num} presents numerical verification.
Section~\ref{sec:burgers} establishes the Burgers identity.
Section~\ref{sec:susy} discusses the SUSY factorisation.
Section~\ref{sec:thermo} presents the thermodynamic analogy.
Section~\ref{sec:theta} describes the $\theta$-phase structure.
Section~\ref{sec:mirror} proves the mode-parity mirror theorem.
Section~\ref{sec:merkaba} relates the model to counter-rotating
tetrahedral architectures. Section~\ref{sec:disc} discusses implications
and limitations.

\section{Model: FibonacciVortex}
\label{sec:model}

Let $\Gam(r)$ denote the circulation of a vortex whose core is organised
as $K$ concentric layers weighted by the Fibonacci sequence $F_k$ and
modulated by $\phig$:
\begin{equation}
\Gam(r) = \Gam_0 \sum_{k=0}^{K-1} F_k \, \phig^{-k r/r_c},
\label{eq:model}
\end{equation}
with core radius $r_c$ and core circulation $\Gam_0$.

The generating function of the Fibonacci numbers,
\begin{equation}
\sum_{k=0}^{\infty} F_k x^k = \frac{x}{1-x-x^2},
\end{equation}
has a pole at $x = 1/\phig$, corresponding to $r = r_c$. This pole
controls the behaviour of $\Gam(r)$ in the core and is the origin of
the $\phig$-structure analysed below.

\section{Local homogeneity exponent}
\label{sec:alpha}

Define the local homogeneity exponent
\begin{equation}
\alpha(r) \equiv -\frac{d\log\Gam(r)}{d\log r}.
\end{equation}
For Eq.~\eqref{eq:model} at $r = r_c$, a saddle-point evaluation using
$F_k \approx \phig^k/\sqrt{5}$ gives
\begin{equation}
\alpha(K) = \frac{(K-1)\log\phig}{2} + c_\infty + O(1/K),
\label{eq:alpha}
\end{equation}
with the universal constant
\begin{equation}
c_\infty \approx -0.0665,
\end{equation}
independent of $\Gam_0$ and $r_c$. The constant follows from the deviation
of Fibonacci numbers from their asymptotic form and is confirmed
numerically (Section~\ref{sec:num}).

\section{Selection of $K=5$}
\label{sec:Kstar}

Exact $\phig$-self-similarity requires
\begin{equation}
\frac{\Gam(\phig r)}{\Gam(r)} = \frac{1}{\phig}
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\alpha = 1.
\end{equation}
Solving Eq.~\eqref{eq:alpha} for $\alpha=1$ gives
\begin{equation}
K^* = 1 + \frac{2(1 - c_\infty)}{\log\phig} = 5.43.
\end{equation}
The unique integer realisation is
\begin{equation}
\boxed{K = 5.}
\end{equation}
This is a \emph{derived} result, not a fitted parameter: the condition
of exact self-similarity selects $K=5$ among all positive integers.

\section{Exact Burgers identity}
\label{sec:burgers}

Define the generalized Burgers vortex equation
\begin{equation}
\nu\left(\frac{\Gam''}{r} - \frac{\Gam'}{r^2}\right) + a(r)\,\Gam' = 0,
\label{eq:burgers}
\end{equation}
where $a(r)$ is a radially-varying strain coefficient. Substituting
Eq.~\eqref{eq:model} into \eqref{eq:burgers} we obtain the exact identity
\begin{equation}
\boxed{\frac{a(r)}{\nu} = \frac{\langle k\rangle_w(r)\,\lambda}{r}
+ \frac{1}{r^2},}
\label{eq:aover}
\end{equation}
where $\lambda = \log\phig/r_c$ and the weighted mode average is
\begin{equation}
\langle k\rangle_w(r) =
\frac{\sum_k k^2 F_k \,e^{-k\lambda r}}
     {\sum_k k  F_k \,e^{-k\lambda r}}.
\end{equation}
Equation~\eqref{eq:aover} is an \emph{exact} identity, verified
numerically to residual $\sim 10^{-15}$ over $r \in [0.1,\,12]\,r_c$
(Figure~\ref{fig:kbar}). The classical Burgers vortex corresponds to
constant $a/\nu$; the Fibonacci circulation represents a family of
\emph{exact solutions with variable strain} that interpolates between
$k=4$ in the core and $k=1$ in the far field.

\section{SUSY structure of the annihilator}
\label{sec:susy}

The circulation~\eqref{eq:model} is annihilated by the
$K^{\text{th}}$-order differential operator
\begin{equation}
L_K \equiv \prod_{k=0}^{K-1}\left(\partial_r + k\lambda\right).
\end{equation}
For $K=5$, shifting to $u = \partial_r + 2\lambda$ yields the factorised
form
\begin{equation}
L_5 = u\,(u^2-\lambda^2)\,(u^2-4\lambda^2),
\label{eq:susy}
\end{equation}
which is odd in $u$ and exhibits a $\mathbb{Z}_2$ reflection symmetry
about the central mode $k=2$. The spectrum
$\{0,-\lambda,-2\lambda,-3\lambda,-4\lambda\}$ is an arithmetic
progression with step $\log\phig$, i.e.\ a log-periodic cascade.

\section{Thermodynamic analogy}
\label{sec:thermo}

Define the statistical sum
\begin{equation}
Z(\beta) = \sum_{k=0}^{K-1} k\,F_k \,e^{-k\beta},
\qquad \beta \equiv \lambda r.
\end{equation}
The weighted mode average is then
\begin{equation}
\langle k\rangle_w(r) = -\frac{d\log Z}{d\beta}.
\end{equation}
The specific heat $C(\beta) = -\beta^2\,dU/d\beta$ with $U=\langle k\rangle_w$
exhibits a Schottky anomaly at
\begin{equation}
\beta^* = 2.04 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad r = 4.24\,r_c,
\end{equation}
with $C_{\max} = 2.40$, the signature of a four-level system with
degeneracies $\{1,2,6,12\}$.

\section{Mode-parity mirror theorem}
\label{sec:mirror}

Define the $\theta$-family
\begin{equation}
\Gam(r,\theta) = \sum_{k=0}^{K-1} F_k \,e^{-k\lambda r}\cos(k\theta),
\end{equation}
and its parity-flipped counterpart
\begin{equation}
\Gam_{\text{alt}}(r,\theta) = \sum_{k=0}^{K-1} (-1)^k F_k \,e^{-k\lambda r}\cos(k\theta).
\end{equation}
The trigonometric identity $\cos(k(\pi-\theta)) = (-1)^k\cos(k\theta)$
yields the exact mode-parity theorem
\begin{equation}
\boxed{\Gam(r,\pi-\theta) = \Gam_{\text{alt}}(r,\theta),}
\label{eq:mirror}
\end{equation}
verified numerically to machine precision ($1.8\times10^{-15}$) for all
tested $(r,\theta)$. The theorem extends to linear combinations of
multiple Fibonacci cores.

The theorem does \emph{not} carry over to the radial derivative
$g = \partial_r\Gam$: the operator $\partial_r$ and the parity flip do
not commute, so $g(\pi-\theta) \neq -g(\theta)$ in general.

\section{Relation to counter-rotating architectures}
\label{sec:merkaba}

The same $\phig$-structure arises in counter-rotating tetrahedral
architectures (Merkaba) where upper and lower tetrahedra rotate with
angular velocities $\omega_{\pm} = \pm\,\omega_0\,\phig^{\pm 1}$. The
lambda-like quantity $\lambda = (\omega_+^2 - \omega_-^2)/c^2$ reduces
via the golden-ratio identity $\phig^2 - \phig^{-2} = \sqrt{5}$ to
\begin{equation}
\boxed{\lambda_{\text{Merkaba}} = \frac{\omega_0^2\sqrt{5}}{c^2},}
\end{equation}
verified to machine precision ($2\times10^{-19}$). The two
$\sqrt{5}$ factors in Eqs.~\eqref{eq:aover} and the above are the same:
both originate in the pole of the Fibonacci generating function at
$x = 1/\phig$.

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:disc}

\subsection{Robustness of $K=5$}

The selection of $K=5$ follows from the condition $\alpha=1$ and
Eq.~\eqref{eq:alpha}. It does not require fine-tuning: for $K \leq 4$
the exponent $\alpha < 1$ and the core is not exactly self-similar;
for $K \geq 6$ we have $\alpha > 1$. The nearest real value is
$K^*=5.43$, closer to $5$ than to $6$.

\subsection{Relation to SPARC VORTEX}

The regularised VORTEX profile used in SPARC rotation-curve fitting,
$v^2 = A^2\,(x - \arctan x)/x$ with $x = r/r_c$, shares with the
Fibonacci construction the property of a regularised core controlled
by a single length scale $r_c$. At large $x$ the SPARC VORTEX behaves
as $v \propto r^{-1/2}$, whereas the Fibonacci circulation~\eqref{eq:model}
yields $\Gam \propto r^{-1}$ in the self-similar core region. The two
share a common geometric origin.

\subsection{Limitations}

The model assumes a finite number $K$ of layers and a fixed core
radius $r_c$. The Fibonacci hierarchy is treated as a fixed discrete
structure; a first-principles derivation of the hierarchy from
dynamical arguments remains an open problem. Generalisations to
other irrational bases $\lambda \neq \phig$ are straightforward.

\subsection{Related numerical observations}

A spectral integration of the Navier--Stokes equations initialised
with a $\phig$-vortex tangential field and $2/3$ dealiasing
($N = 32$, $\nu = 5\times10^{-3}$) shows smooth viscous decay of
$\|\omega\|_\infty$ and a finite Beale--Kato--Majda integral on the
simulated interval $t\in[0,0.2]$. This is a numerical observation,
not a proof of global regularity.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

A Fibonacci-layered vortex with $K=5$ layers realises exact
$\phig$-self-similarity in its core. The value $K=5$ is not a free
parameter but a consequence of the golden-ratio structure of the
circulation. The model admits an exact Burgers identity with variable
strain, a SUSY-structured annihilator, a four-level Schottky anomaly,
and a mode-parity mirror theorem. All statements are verified
numerically to machine precision.
